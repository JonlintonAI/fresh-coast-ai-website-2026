A clear starting point before implementation

Before buying tools or commissioning a build, decide what problem you want to solve and what a useful result would look like. Discovery, assessment, and architecture give that decision a defined scope and a known fee.

What the engagement covers

  • Discovery: Your goals, workflows, team needs, and current systems.
  • Assessment: Opportunities, data readiness, constraints, and risks.
  • Architecture: The proposed solution, systems and data flows, requirements, and an implementation plan.

We agree the scope, responsibilities, and schedule before starting. The aim is a recommendation your team can evaluate and a practical basis for deciding what comes next.

What comes after discovery

The discovery fee does not include building or operating the solution. If you choose to proceed, implementation receives its own scope and quote based on the agreed requirements. Ongoing advisory and support are also quoted separately.

Software subscriptions, usage charges, integrations, testing, training, and support can all affect the total cost of a solution. Ask which costs are included in a proposal and which you would pay separately.

Compare scope before comparing quotes

A strategy document and a working integration are different deliverables. Compare the work, ownership, acceptance criteria, and support commitments before comparing fees. A broad market range cannot tell you what your project needs.

  • What will we receive at the end of this phase?
  • What access and input will our team need to provide?
  • What is excluded, and how are changes approved?
  • What decisions should this work enable?
  • What would implementation and ongoing operation involve?
