## Why the current site feels generic

The opening gives its greatest emphasis to a broad promise: less busywork, clearer answers, usable AI. A long introduction, two links, and a small portrait complete the hero. These are understandable, but many consultants could use the same composition and promise. The next evidence section shows Stanton's logo and a quotation; the tangible digest remains deeper in the case study. Visitors encounter descriptions of practical implementation before seeing its output.

Across the page, a serif headline, teal italic ending, tiny uppercase mono label, pale background, rounded button, and generous padding recur. Each device is reasonable individually. Their repetition gives a client story, a founder belief, a service explanation, and a sales invitation similar visual importance. Services repeats five large numbered rows with much the same structure. The warmer copy has more character than the layout expresses.

This is an editorial assessment of hierarchy and repetition, not evidence of AI provenance. Large headings and serif fonts also appear on distinctive professional sites. Changing fonts alone would not address the delayed proof, small human imagery, or repetitive reading rhythm.

Use primarily sans typography, compact descriptive labels, ruled divisions, and a substantial Stanton digest. Pair the opening copy with the output instead of a portrait sidebar. Explain selected details using approved facts: code calculates figures, AI summarizes priorities, checks constrain the output, and the client team operates the system. Keep the illustrative sample-data label attached to the artifact.

The next section supplies founder context and the CultureCon quotation. Present common business problems as a compact index, with clear links to the relevant Services sections. A small diagram may explain the approved case-study data path if it helps the reader understand it. It should depict that implementation, not imply a universal architecture or a product sold to every buyer.

On desktop, leave enough width to read the output. On mobile, show a readable excerpt and a link to the full case study rather than shrinking a wide table into illegibility. A table may need its own scroll region, but the surrounding page should reflow. The direction's character comes from useful information and precise annotation, not fake dashboards, decorative technical labels, or invented performance figures.

## Research limits and validation

Stanford's credibility guidance supports verifiable information, recognizable people, clear contact details, and design appropriate to purpose. It does not establish that a particular FCAI palette will produce more leads. NN/g's 1997 web-reading study supports concise, scannable, objective writing; its usability findings are old and must not be presented as current conversion estimates. Its later layer-cake research supports descriptive headings that help readers identify relevant content. [Stanford credibility guidelines](https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines/index.html), [NN/g web reading](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/), [NN/g scanning patterns](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/)

Baymard's form-field findings concern ecommerce checkout. Their limited transferable lesson is to reduce unnecessary visible decisions; they do not predict inquiry-form lift. W3C supports clear success and error notifications and reflow at an equivalent width of 320 CSS pixels, with exceptions for content such as data tables that genuinely requires two-dimensional layout. [Baymard form research](https://baymard.com/research-articles/checkout-flow-average-form-fields), [W3C form notifications](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/notifications/), [W3C reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)

Compare both homepage drafts with the same approved copy. At desktop, 390px, and 320px widths, check reading order, image integrity, sample-label adjacency, overflow, focus visibility, keyboard navigation, and meaningful link labels. Test booking and inquiry separately through their actual completion states when authorized; local redirects alone do not prove a calendar reservation or email receipt. Measure contrast before making compliance claims. Ask representative readers to explain the offer, identify the supporting evidence, and find the appropriate next step. Record misunderstandings and revise before spreading the selected system across templates.
