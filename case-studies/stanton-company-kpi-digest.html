02 / The problem

Good records.
No weekly answer.

Stanton's account teams already kept good records. Every client had a tracker in Google Sheets, KPI targets set at the start of the engagement, and a priorities email sent by the account manager on Monday morning.

Each tracker answered for its own account. Leadership needed one weekly answer: are we on pace against what we promised each client? Seeing every account side by side meant opening each tracker and doing the math by hand, which could take hours.

Amy Stanton wanted that picture every Monday, built so her team kept working the way they already did and owned the result.

03 / The build

Meet KPI Bob.

FCAI built a weekly digest the team named KPI Bob. It reads each client's tracker and the master KPI sheet, then works out where every account stands against its goals for its own goal period.

Accounts are grouped as On track, Watch, or Needs attention, each with a one-line reason. Early-stage accounts show as Building. The digest also checks whether the week's priorities address the KPIs that need the most help.

It sends Monday and Tuesday at 9:30 a.m. Pacific. Monday is the read. Tuesday picks up late inputs and holidays, and shows what changed since the day before.

Leadership gets two formats: a short email listing only the accounts that need a look, and a full browser view with every account and every KPI.

04 / Under the hood

Familiar tools.
Clear checks and ownership.

The data path

The only new habit is one CC on an email the team already sends. Account managers keep updating their trackers and copy the digest's mailbox on their Monday priorities email.

A sheet lists every account, tracker, status, and target. The digest reads live from those files on every run, so fixing the sheet fixes the next digest.

The numbers check

Ordinary code calculates every figure, band, and flag. AI reads the week's priorities and writes the bottom-line summary.

Every number in AI-written text is checked against the computed values before sending. If the AI is unavailable, a plainer template ships instead.

The ownership model

Everything runs in Stanton's Google Workspace account and Google Cloud project, billed to Stanton and administered by their IT partner. The AI uses Stanton's own account, where prompts are not used to train Google's models.

Stanton received the code, an operations guide, an IT infrastructure guide, a failure playbook, and a one-button rerun. Run cost is a single Workspace seat plus metered AI usage.

Lesson learned

Read the whole workbook.

FCAI's first design read only each tracker's current tab. That would have lost the cumulative picture as the months rolled over. FCAI rebuilt it to read the whole workbook, and that is how it runs today.
